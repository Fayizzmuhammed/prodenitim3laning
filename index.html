You might be scrubbing the right places while missing the part that matters most: the balance of bacteria living in your mouth.

Sound familiar?

  • Your breath goes stale a couple of hours after brushing
  • Your gums look a little puffy or pink when you floss
  • You use strong mouthwash, but the "clean" feeling doesn't last
  • You do everything right and still dread your next check-up

Your mouth is an ecosystem, not a surface

Hundreds of bacterial species live in your mouth. Most are harmless or helpful. When the helpful ones are crowded out, the troublemakers have more room to thrive.

That's why researchers have been taking a closer look at the oral microbiome. A 2021 article in Nature covered how this research is challenging some long-held ideas in dentistry.

Harsh antibacterial products don't pick sides. They wipe out the good bacteria along with the bad.

So a growing number of people are trying a different approach: putting good bacteria back in, and keeping brushing and flossing as they are.
